Flow-OPD: On-Policy Distillation for Flow Matching Models

Zhen Fang1*   ·   Wenxuan Huang2*†   ·   Yu Zeng1   ·   Yiming Zhao1   ·   Shuang Chen3   ·   Kaituo Feng4   ·   Yunlong Lin4   ·   Jie Liu4   ·   Lin Chen1   ·   Zehui Chen1   ·   Shaosheng Cao5†   ·   Feng Zhao1
1USTC  ·  2Project Leader  ·  3UCLA  ·  4CUHK  ·  5Xiaohongshu     * Equal Contribution    † Corresponding Author

We identify two critical bottlenecks in multi-task Flow Matching training: reward sparsity and gradient interference. Flow-OPD integrates On-Policy Distillation into the Flow Matching pipeline, replacing sparse scalar rewards with dense, trajectory-level, multi-teacher vector field supervision. Evaluated on SD-3.5-Medium, Flow-OPD achieves +18pt average improvement over vanilla GRPO and surpasses individual teacher models on OCR and DeQA.

01 — Abstract

Motivation: Why Multi-Task Flow Matching Fails

Standard GRPO works in single-task settings but catastrophically degrades in multi-task settings. We analyze the root causes and propose a principled solution.

Reward Sparsity

Scalar-valued rewards (e.g., a single OCR accuracy score) compress high-dimensional image space into a single number. The model receives insufficient gradient signal to adjust thousands of dimensions simultaneously.

Information Bottleneck

Gradient Interference

Jointly optimizing heterogeneous objectives (text rendering, aesthetic quality, human preference) within shared parameters causes divergent gradients to conflict ($\langle \nabla_\theta J_k, \nabla_\theta J_1\rangle < 0$), producing a zero-sum seesaw effect.

Catastrophic Forgetting

Flow-OPD Solution

Replace sparse scalar rewards with dense, trajectory-level, multi-teacher vector field supervision. ~10pt average improvement over GRPO baseline; the unified student surpasses individual teachers in OCR and DeQA.

02

On-Policy Sampling (SDE)

Convert the deterministic ODE into a SDE for stochastic exploration. Sample G trajectories per prompt to generate on-policy marginal distributions.

03

Multi-Teacher Dense Labeling

Each teacher acts as a Generative Reward Model (GRM), returning a full vector field $v_{\phi_k}$ rather than a scalar. Dynamic routing $\alpha_k$ selects domain experts.

04

KL Reward + PPO Clipping

Shared isotropic covariance reduces KL divergence to an $L_2$ vector field distance. PPO-style clipping stabilizes training against reward exploitation.

Flow Matching Formulation
Probability flow ODE (deterministic):   $$\frac{d x_t}{dt} = v_\theta(x_t, t, c)$$
Stochastic sampling (SDE) for on-policy exploration:   $$dx_t = \Big[ v_\theta(x_t, t) - \frac{(1-t)x_t}{2t} \Big] dt + \sigma_t \, dw$$
Multi-Teacher Dense Supervision
GRM aggregation:   $$v_{\text{target}}(x_t, t, c) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \alpha_k(c, x_t) \cdot v_{\phi_k}(x_t, t, c)$$
Dense reward (vector field $L_2$ distance):   $$r_t^{(i)} = -w(t) \cdot \big\| \bar{v}_\theta(x_t^{(i)}, t, c) - v_{\text{target}}(x_t^{(i)}, t, c) \big\|_2^2$$
KL Divergence as $L_2$ Distance
Shared isotropic covariance $\sigma_t^2 \Delta t \, I$ reduces KL analytically to:   $$D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_\theta \, \| \, \pi_{\text{target}}) = \frac{\Delta t}{2} \cdot \big\| v_\theta - v_{\text{target}} \big\|_2^2$$
PPO clipped surrogate objective:   $$J(\theta) \approx \frac{1}{BG} \sum \min\!\Big( \rho_{t,i,j}(\theta) \cdot r_{t,i,j}^{\text{OPD}},\; \mathrm{clip}(\rho_{t,i,j}(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon) \cdot r_{t,i,j}^{\text{OPD}} \Big)$$

Manifold Anchor Regularization (MAR)

To prevent aesthetic degradation from aggressive functional optimization, Flow-OPD introduces a task-agnostic aesthetic teacher (optimized via DeQA) providing full-data supervision. The total loss combines the policy objective with a KL regularization term:

Total Loss
KL regularization:   $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Total}}(\theta) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{Policy}}(\theta) + \lambda \cdot \mathbb{E}_{c,\, t,\, x_t \sim \rho_t^\theta}\Big[ w(t) \cdot \big\| v_\theta(x_t, t, c) - v_{\text{aesthetic}}(x_t, t, c) \big\|_2^2 \Big]$$

MAR resolves two failure modes of vanilla GRPO: (1) background mode collapse from overfitting to monotonous reward landscapes, and (2) semantic redundancy from coarse scalar reward granularity.

Why MAR Works

The KL regularization from the aesthetic teacher acts as a continuous elastic anchor, decoupling functional alignment (per-task vector fields) from stylistic preservation (aesthetic manifold). Without MAR, the model suffers from background mode collapse (monotonous environments) and semantic redundancy (identical features across entities).

04 — Analysis

Why Flow-OPD Works

Three mechanisms jointly resolve the reward sparsity and gradient interference bottlenecks.

Cold Start Prevents Trajectory Divergence

Without initialization, early rollout trajectories diverge from the teacher's high-quality manifold. Model Merging positions the student in a high-competence parameter region without additional gradient interference.

Dense Supervision Eliminates Reward Sparsity

Each teacher provides a full vector field at every diffusion timestep. The L2 distance between student and teacher vector fields gives dense per-step rewards, fully utilizing the high-dimensional action space.

Dynamic Routing Prevents Gradient Interference

The routing αk(c, xt) activates only relevant task experts. Orthogonal task gradients are no longer summed into a conflicting direction — each task contributes independently.

MAR Decouples Function from Style

The policy loss optimizes functional alignment while MAR preserves aesthetic quality. This elastic separation prevents reward hacking: the model can improve task performance without sacrificing generative diversity.

06 — Limitations & Future Work

Honest Assessment

Teacher Ceiling

When specialized teachers fail to synthesize semantically correct images, errors propagate through dense supervisory signals, limiting the student ceiling. Weak teachers cap student performance.

Architectural Homogeneity

Teacher and student must share the same architecture for fine-grained, step-wise vector field supervision. Cross-architecture distillation remains an open challenge.

Future Directions

Co-evolutionary Distillation

Iteratively refine teachers and students together, potentially breaking the teacher ceiling bottleneck through mutual improvement.

Self-Distillation

Use the student itself as a multi-expert ensemble via task-conditioned routing, boosting performance without external teachers.

Cross-Architecture Distillation

Bridge heterogeneous model architectures (e.g., DiT vs. U-Net) through shared continuous representations.
