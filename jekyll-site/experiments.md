        <h1>Experiments</h1>
    </div>

    <div class="content-section">
        <h2>Setup</h2>
        <p>
            Following Flow-GRPO, we evaluate on four tasks: <strong>GenEval</strong> (compositional image generation), <strong>OCR</strong> (visual text rendering), <strong>PickScore</strong> (human preference alignment), and <strong>DeQA</strong> (image quality). All training and test data strictly follow the Flow-GRPO splits.
        </p>
        <p>
            <strong>Compute:</strong> Training on 4 nodes (8× H800 GPUs each) for ~50 hours. Evaluation on a single 8× H800 node.
        </p>
        <p>
            <strong>Base Model:</strong> Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium (SD-3.5-M)
        </p>
        <p>
            <strong>Hyperparameters:</strong> Sampling timestep \(T=10\), evaluation timestep \(T=40\), group size \(G=24\), noise level \(a=0.7\), image resolution 512. MAR KL ratio \(\beta=0.02\). LoRA with \(\alpha=64, r=32\).
        </p>

        <h2>Benchmarks</h2>
        <div class="card">
            <h3>GenEval</h3>
            <p>Assesses the model's ability to handle complex semantic compositions — object counting, spatial relations, and attribute binding. Prompt weighting: Position:Counting:Attribute Binding:Colors:Two Objects:Single Object = 7:5:3:1:1:0.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="card">
            <h3>OCR (Visual Text Rendering)</h3>
            <p>Evaluates precise text synthesis within images. Uses 20K training prompts generated by GPT-4o with template: <em>"A sign that says 'text'"</em>.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="card">
            <h3>DeQA (Image Quality)</h3>
            <p>Monitors overall visual fidelity using the DrawBench dataset, ensuring high aesthetic standards and structural integrity across diverse prompts.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="card">
            <h3>PickScore (Human Preference)</h3>
            <p>Derived from large-scale human-annotated pairwise comparisons, evaluating semantic accuracy and visual superiority according to human-centric benchmarks.</p>
        </div>

        <h2>Baselines</h2>
        <p>We compare against two categories:</p>
        <ol>
            <li><strong>Monolithic-Reward GRPO</strong> (<em>GRPO-[reward name]</em>): Fine-tuned using Flow-GRPO on a single reward objective.</li>
            <li><strong>Hybrid-Reward GRPO</strong> (<em>GRPO-Mix</em>): Weighted reward combination with GenEval:OCR:PickScore = 3:1:1.</li>
        </ol>

        <h2>Ablation Studies</h2>

        <h3>Cold Start Ablation</h3>
        <p>
            Between the two cold-start regimes: <strong>Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)</strong> serves as a widely adopted and highly scalable strategy. <strong>Model Merging</strong> optimally leverages available homogeneous teachers for superior functional alignment without additional training costs.
        </p>
        <p>
            Flow-OPD consistently outperforms both from-scratch and cold-started multi-task GRPO. While GRPO converges to sub-optimal states due to inter-task conflicts caused by sparse scalar rewards, Flow-OPD leverages dense multi-expert supervision to resolve gradient interference.
        </p>

        <h3>OOD Generalization on T2I-CompBench</h3>
        <p>
            Flow-OPD demonstrates superior out-of-distribution generalization compared to multi-task GRPO, achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple compositional metrics. When initialized from the identical cold-start baseline, standard GRPO suffers from catastrophic forgetting in specific capability dimensions (e.g., shape rendering, 3D spatial relations). In contrast, Flow-OPD effectively mitigates these regression issues.
        </p>

        <h3>Manifold Anchor Regularization</h3>
        <p>
            Vanilla GRPO-based optimization often triggers <em>background mode collapse</em> (overfitting to monotonous environments) and <em>semantic redundancy</em> (identical features across entities due to coarse reward granularity). MAR resolves these by anchoring optimization to a high-fidelity manifold, balancing structural diversity with precise semantic adherence.
        </p>

        <h2>Comparison with DiffusionNFT</h2>
        <p>
            DiffusionNFT introduces an online RL framework that integrates reward feedback into the forward diffusion process. Despite competitive benchmark scores, it has two critical limitations: fundamental incompatibility with Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), and pronounced reward hacking — generating malformed hands, extraneous objects, and over-smoothed, plastic-like textures.
        </p>
        <p>
            By leveraging continuous-time dense multi-expert supervision and task-style decoupling, Flow-OPD effectively circumvents these reward hacking behaviors, achieving significantly higher Qwen-VL scores (4.05 vs. 3.74).
        </p>

        <h2>Limitations</h2>
        <div class="warning-box">
            <h4>Teacher Performance Ceiling</h4>
            <p>When specialized teachers fail to synthesize semantically correct images, these inaccuracies propagate through dense supervisory signals, introducing noise into the distillation objective and hindering the student's ability to transcend teacher limitations.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="warning-box">
            <h4>Architectural Homogeneity Requirement</h4>
            <p>Flow-OPD requires architectural homogeneity between the teacher and student models to facilitate fine-grained, step-wise supervision. Future directions include: Co-evolutionary Distillation, Self-Distillation, and Cross-Vocabulary Distillation.</p>
        </div>
